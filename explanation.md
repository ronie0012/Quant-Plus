## 1. The problem in the market

Researching a stock in India today looks like this:

- **Prices and charts** in one app (broker or TradingView)
- **News** in another app
- **Fundamentals** on screener websites
- **AI opinions** in a chatbot that answers from memory, with no sources
- **Trading notes** in a spreadsheet or a notebook
- **Risk**… in the user's head
- **Practice trading** in a simulator that changes a single balance number, charges nothing, and teaches nothing
- **Review** — almost never happens, because nothing remembers the context of the decision

Nobody connects these. The result: people trade on vibes, screenshot charts, trust chatbot answers blindly, never write down a thesis, never check whether their *process* was good — only whether they won or lost money.

**Quant Plus was built to close that gap.**

## 2. The core idea — one connected loop

The whole product is organised around a single loop:

```
Observe → Investigate → Challenge → Understand risk → Practise → Record → Review → Learn
```

Five navigation areas match this loop:

| Area | What it answers |
|---|---|
| **Home** | "What is happening today and what should I do next?" |
| **Discover** | "What is the market showing me? What is this stock really about?" |
| **Portfolio** | "What do I own, what did it cost me, and how much risk am I carrying?" |
| **Review** | "What happened to my decisions, and what did I learn?" |
| **More** | System tools — settings, data health, help, roadmap |

Plus a dedicated **Learn & Practise** wing for beginners.

Everything a user touches belongs to a step of the loop. That's the design principle: **the evidence, the reasoning, the risk, the practice and the review are one workflow — not five products.**

## 3. Home — your "today" page

### The dashboard
Home is organised around three honest questions: **Money** (wallet + portfolio snapshot), **Market** (index tape, market overview, sentiment, calendar), and **Next actions** — a feed automatically built from your open plans, triggered alerts, holdings with events coming up, and fresh research sessions. Instead of staring at widgets, you get an answer to *"what should I pay attention to right now?"*

### Onboarding that actually onboards
New users get a concrete first-session checklist: fund the wallet → make one paper decision (or a deliberate watch-only decision) → set one price alert → review that decision. It completes itself from your real activity, not from clicking "Next".

### Workspace modes
The dashboard can be tuned per persona — **Learner, Trader, Researcher, Portfolio manager** — with selectable panel layouts, plus a universal **command search** (Ctrl-K style) that searches *pages, symbols, evidence claims, orders, plans, news and saved research* in one box.

### Focus, calm and low-bandwidth modes
The topbar and settings support Focus mode, calm display density, low-bandwidth mode, and a review gate — the product is designed to be usable at night, on mobile data, and during market hours without overwhelming the user.

## 4. Learn & Practise — the school wings

### Learning path
An actual curriculum — investor profile, risk capacity and tolerance, market concepts, AI-assisted tutoring with concept checks, company/IPO simulation — not a PDF dump. New participants learn how evidence, risk, costs and liquidity affect a decision **before** touching capital.

### Investor DNA
A short questionnaire that builds each user's risk profile — appetite, tolerance, capacity. The rest of the product treats this DNA seriously: sizing suggestions, confirmation thresholds and behavioural protections are calibrated against who you actually are, not who the app wants you to be.

### Be the Company
Unique learning simulation: the user **becomes a company**. Issue an IPO, watch shares circulate, experience dilution, understand why share prices move — by living it rather than reading it. This demystifies "what is a share" better than any textbook page.

### AI tutor
Concept-by-concept tutoring with quiz-style mastery checks. Teaching is the copilot's other job; the same evidence discipline applies to education answers.

## 5. Discover — observing the market

### Market pulse
Provider-backed index behaviour: NIFTY and Bank Nifty quotes, index history over multiple time ranges, breadth, drivers, and — importantly — if the index feed is unavailable, the page **keeps the NIFTY 50 board usable** and states plainly that the index view is not available. No invented numbers.

### The NIFTY 50 board
A full constituent board with search, sector filtering, sorting, per-row **source labels, timestamps and freshness states** (live / delayed / closed / stale), breadth counters, and strongest/weakest movers. Every row opens the full stock workspace. Every quote answers *who said this, and when*.

### Watchlists
Persistent, with streaming current quotes, All/Gainers/Decliners filters, and a view for any selected instrument. Missing quotes remain visibly *unknown* rather than blank or zero.

### Screener
Filters the universe by sector, provider-backed momentum, volatility and the research verdict. Saved screens carry the **formula version they were created with** — load one under a newer formula and the app warns you the factors changed. Screeners elsewhere silently reinterpret saved filters; here the past stays comparable.

### Event Radar
Everything scheduled that can move your holdings: exchange calendar events, classified news, expiries, computed T+1 settlement dates, holdings-only filtering, historical reaction where data allows, and — the thoughtful part — whether the event was **knowable at the time you traded**.

### Market Context (Regime Map)
Places today into historical context: observable market features, the closest historical states ("analogs"), what came next across different horizons, breadth/news/sector context, and honest labelling when institutional flow data (FII/DII) is unavailable. It is presented as **context, never as a forecast**.

### Research briefs
A research terminal that combines market intelligence and completed analyses into one filtered reading surface, linked to the full symbol research.

### Option chain — the honest gate
There is an Options route, but it renders as **explicitly unavailable** until observed chains, verified lot/expiry rules and validated Greeks exist. It is kept deep-linkable and *out of the sidebar* deliberately. Very few products in any market publish their own unfinished capabilities to users; Quant Plus makes gates visible rather than hiding them.

## 6. The stock workspace — the heart of the product

One page, one symbol, the whole story:

### Quote header
Price, change, **source, provider timestamp, session state and freshness** — live, closed, delayed, stale, or waiting. Missing values render as a dash, never as fake zeros. The current research verdict sits next to the price, so observation and opinion stay connected.

### Professional chart
Candlesticks / line / area over 1D–1Y ranges, real validated OHLCV bars, volume, MA20/EMA50/RSI14 indicators with honest warm-up states, crosshair, zoom, pan, keyboard navigation, and two overlays almost no chart in the retail market has:

- **Event markers** — NSE exchange and calendar events drawn onto the price history.
- **Your own decisions and journal entries** plotted on the chart for the same symbol — your past self is visible right where the price was.

Browser-local **chart notes** persist under each symbol.

### Technical & fundamental facts
Momentum, volatility, RSI, market cap, P/E, dividend yield, beta, 52-week range — shown only when the provider actually returned them.

### Market microstructure panel
Bid/ask, displayed depth quantities, circuits, traded volume, plus provider fields — with the snapshot age identified and an explicit note when the fallback source does not provide five-level depth.

### Stock news intelligence
Not just headlines: a weighted sentiment score with **positive/neutral/negative counts, confidence, data-quality rating, high-impact stories, top drivers, and whether price confirmed or diverged from the news tone**. The score is labelled as heuristic — never as a probability.

### Decision Canvas
The in-page handoff between thinking and acting: **Evidence → Thesis → Risk → Paper trade or Watch-only → Review**. It shows the current thesis, the counter-case, and the quote evidence, then links to create, continue or review a plan.

### Paper order ticket
BUY/SELL, MARKET/LIMIT/STOP, quantity, stop loss, target, holding period, **linking to the open decision plan**, and the live risk preview with warnings — before any order is placed. When the market is closed, an explicit "last-session practice" mode is offered so a learner can still execute against the last real snapshot.

## 7. The AI research committee

This is the part of the platform nobody in the Indian retail market offers: research produced by a **visible team of specialist agents**, in stages a user can read.

### Why a committee?
Price may improve while fundamentals weaken. News may sound great while the market reacts badly. A stock may look attractive alone while your portfolio is already overweight the same risk. One blended AI answer hides all of that. A committee **preserves disagreement**.

### How a research session runs

```
Ask for research on a symbol (with a data cutoff)
    ↓
Five specialist analysts read the available evidence, in parallel:
      Technicals · Fundamentals · News · Macro · Events
    ↓
Every output is validated — schema, numbers, cited evidence, cutoff
    ↓
Bull analyst vs Bear analyst argue from the evidence ledger
    ↓
Research manager synthesises a structured thesis + conviction
    ↓
Devil's advocate attacks the thesis — repeat risks vs new contradictions,
      and a verdict: healthy or broken
    ↓
Thesis version is recorded (like a signed draft of your opinion)
    ↓
Trader constructs a research-only proposal
    ↓
Three risk reviewers weigh it: Aggressive · Conservative · Neutral
    ↓
Portfolio manager issues the final RESEARCH-ONLY decision
```

### What the user actually sees
Not a paragraph of confidence — a set of inspectable artifacts: five analyst reports, both sides of the argument, the challenge findings, each risk perspective, the final rationale, the evidence cutoff, and the full stage timeline. Research is checkpointed: a completed session is stored, can be re-read instantly, and refreshes separately so the original result **stays visible while new work runs**.

### Where to see it in the product
- **Research / AI debate (Committee)** — stored sessions and their verdicts.
- **Research trace (Agent Activity)** — the timeline of stages the system went through.
- **Research history** — every past analysis.
- **Verdict details** — the decision queue with verdicts, risk labels, rationale and an *uncalibrated* opinion-strength label (the product refuses to dress opinion up as probability).

## 8. Quant AI — the evidence-aware copilot

Quant AI is separate from the research committee. It is the conversational assistant, and it is built on a principle almost no chatbot on the market implements end-to-end: **it may only say what it can show.**

### How an answer is produced

```
Your question + your authenticated context
    ↓
Symbol/entity resolution
    ↓
Bounded READ-ONLY evidence collection (typed tools):
      paper account · holdings · risk window · recent orders & audits
      journal · market overview · stock observation · validated history facts
      news tone · saved research
    ↓
Server-side model request (your keys never touch the browser)
    ↓
The candidate answer is buffered on the server
    ↓
Automated claim-checking: every number verified against the cited evidence —
      field · sign · unit · instrument · citation identity
    ↓
Supported answer published with an evidence footer — or an honest ABSTENTION
```

### What this means in practice
- Ask *"why did this stock move?"* and it explains with the market/news/research context it actually retrieved, labelled with source and freshness.
- Ask about your portfolio risk, a proposed trade, a journal entry, or a completed trade — it reviews **your** real account state.
- If the evidence isn't sufficient, it says **"I can't support that"** instead of inventing a number. Invented figures, wrong signs, wrong fields, wrong instruments and fake citations are all caught and withheld before the answer reaches you.

### The most important boundary in the product
**Quant AI is read-only.** It cannot place, amend or cancel an order. Its system prompt enforces this; its tools don't have write access; the deterministic paper-book service owns all money. The model can *advise*; code *decides*. That separation of intelligence from authority is the philosophical spine of the whole platform.

### Where it lives
As a full page with task modes (explain a move, compare evidence, portfolio risk, pre-trade review, journal review, post-trade review), streaming answers, stop/retry, and saved conversations you can search, reload and export — plus a **contextual dock available on every screen**, which automatically attaches the stock you're currently viewing.

## 9. The Ripple Engine

News tells you what happened to **one company**. The Ripple Engine answers the question the rest of the market ignores:

> **"If this event matters, what else moves with it — through what relationship, and how strong is the evidence?"**

### How it works

```
A headline or official event
    ↓
Normalised — source, time, identity, company resolution
    ↓
Classified — which factor, which direction, impact, confidence
    ↓
A verified "ripple origin" is created (dated, recent, never future)
    ↓
The relationship graph is traversed — stocks ↔ sectors ↔ factors ↔
      commodities ↔ currency ↔ rates ↔ macro themes
    ↓
The strongest explainable paths are kept (bounded, no loops)
    ↓
Historical echoes — has a shock like this hit before, and what followed?
    ↓
The map: affected stocks · mechanism · path · evidence quality · freshness
```

### What makes it special
- **Second-order thinking, made inspectable.** A rate cut hits banks — but also lenders, real estate and duration-sensitive names. A commodity spike hits producers *and* their downstream customers. Ordinary news feeds stop at the first ticker mentioned; Ripple follows the chain.
- **Evidence strength is shown, not implied.** Every connection carries direction, mechanism and confidence, and the historical echo only appears when the baseline data genuinely exists — never invented.
- **It is a graph, not a guess.** Connections are explicit relationships, preserving the strongest explainable path so the result stays auditable.

This is one of the clearest "you won't find this next door" features in the product.

## 10. Portfolio — money, orders and risk

### Wallet
Not "you have ₹10,00,000, go wild." A real cash account: deposits and withdrawals with **idempotent intent IDs** (double-clicks can't double-add), a paginated ledger, **settled vs unsettled vs withdrawable cash** (T+1 settlement awareness — you can't withdraw pending sale proceeds), reservations, deployed capital, and realised P&L with charges.

### Portfolio
Holdings marked by the shared provider stream: current vs invested value, unrealised P&L, return, allocation, contribution to today's move, sector exposure, **stale/delayed/missing-quote warnings on each holding**, upcoming holding events, unprotected holdings (no stop/target set), and open decision plans.

### Orders — a ledger, not a list
Complete paginated order history with symbol/status filters, every state (open / filled / partial / cancelled / expired / rejected), fill counts, prices, charges, expiry, server-side protections, amend and cancel, and — this is rare anywhere — **per-order execution audit**: the intent, each fill, the reference price, the fill method (bid/ask vs last-trade+slippage), liquidity limitations, quote timestamp and source, itemised charges, ledger entries, reservations and the full event sequence. Export the JSON. Read exactly what the machine did.

### Order management
Amendments require a fresh quote and version checks (you can't amend against a stale price), and OCO stop/target exits for existing holdings — fill one, the sibling cancels automatically.

### Basket orders
Up to 20 independent legs, evaluated server-side with quote preflight against a copied book, per-leg statuses, and safe retry using original intent IDs — a failed leg never double-fires on retry.

### Alerts
Price and percentage-move alerts with enable/disable, one-shot trigger + re-arm, deletion, full delivery history, and the observed price/change with the provider quote time — so an alert's honesty is checkable.

### Risk Centre
Server-computed, not client-side decoration: day P&L and the **loss window anchored at start of day**, stale-mark handling, cash flows, deterministic stress, configurable max order value, max position %, confirmation threshold, daily loss limit, and a **pause-new-exposure switch**. The design rule that matters: *risk-reducing exits are never blocked* — you can always get out; only *new* risk is restricted.

### Exposure / Portfolio X-Ray
Institutional-style analytics on a personal book: contribution to today's move, VaR, beta, effective positions, drawdown, stress, risk contribution, marginal risk, **liquidity / exit days** (how long to unwind), gap loss, sector weights, correlation clusters, cash/event runway — and a **digital twin**: a slider what-if simulator that shows how the portfolio would look with a change, **without trading anything**.

### Trading Habits (behavioural intelligence)
Consent-based analysis of your own paper history to surface the patterns that actually lose money: sizing up after losses, rapid re-entry (revenge), **moving stops away from risk**, ignoring your own invalidation condition, repeating the same process failure. Configurable protections follow: cooling-off, daily decision limits, drawdown-based sizing caps, and an optional thesis *reminder* before entry (nudges only — ordering never requires a decision plan). All applied to new entries, **never to exits**.

## 11. The paper-trading engine — why it is different

Almost every simulator on the market is a balance number that changes. Quant Plus paper trading is a **financial-grade accounting system** — the kind of engineering exchanges and brokers use — applied to a virtual NSE cash-equity book.

| Reality | How Quant Plus models it |
|---|---|
| **Who owns the price?** | The **server** fetches the execution quote from the provider. Browser-supplied prices are never authoritative — a user (or a hacker) cannot "buy" at a cheaper self-supplied price. |
| **What quote is valid?** | Positive finite price, symbol match, correct provider, valid timestamp, non-stale, non-delayed, **open verified exchange session**. Closed-market snapshots are accepted only under an explicit practice mode. |
| **Where did my fill come from?** | A valid bid/ask spread uses the ask (buy) / bid (sell); otherwise last trade **plus a recorded slippage assumption**. Every fill stores the entire quote observation. |
| **Liquidity** | Displayed top-of-book size limits fills. If displayed liquidity is insufficient → **partial fill**, and the unfilled remainder of a market order is cancelled — never silently filled at an invented quantity. |
| **Money while the order is open** | Buys **reserve cash**; sells **reserve shares**. Cancelled/expired orders release the reservation. |
| **Fees** | Itemised **STT, exchange transaction charges, SEBI fees, stamp duty, GST, brokerage and DP assumptions**, computed in **integer paise** with a dated fee schedule. Unknown charge dates fail closed. |
| **Settlement** | T+1 awareness: settled vs unsettled shares and cash, pending sale proceeds, clearing-day availability rules, withdrawal blocks on unsettled money. |
| **Expired orders** | DAY orders expire at the **verified session close** via the exchange calendar (which itself is hash-verified and fails closed on unverified special sessions). |
| **Protections** | Stop-loss, target and trailing stops persist on the **server** — they keep working when your browser is closed, evaluated by a leased durable worker. OCO: stop and target share one reservation; filling one cancels the sibling. |
| **Retries** | Every mutation carries an **intent ID + fingerprint**. Retrying replays the original response instead of creating a duplicate order; changing the details under the same intent ID is rejected as a conflict. |
| **Self-audit** | Continuous reconciliation: cash = ledger balance, positions = remaining fills, reservations cover open obligations, event sequence unique and monotonic. |

**Why this matters:** practising on a simulator that ignores charges, liquidity, reservations and settlement trains habits that get punished by the real market. Practising on this one trains habits the real market rewards.

## 12. Review — the learning half of the product

Most products stop at execution. Quant Plus treats execution as the **middle** of the story.

### Review Hub
A daily triage queue of decisions that need attention: invalidated theses not yet reflected on, outcomes not reviewed, holding periods elapsed, open linked orders, and unreviewed no-trade decisions. Review stops being optional by default.

### Decision Journal
Every decision saved with its thesis, evidence, risks, invalidation condition, planned exit, reflection, outcome, linked order — and a **decision-time provider quote snapshot, recorded at creation and immutable afterwards**. Months later, you can see exactly what the market looked like when you decided. No other retail tool freezes the decision context like this.

### Decision Plans (Lifecycle)
The full structured plan: expected outcome, thesis, **counter-thesis**, invalidation, evidence items (each with kind, source, publication time and availability time), entry / stop / target / time exit, holding period, quantity, **sizing logic derived from risk**, expected fees and slippage, maximum acceptable loss, market snapshot, risk preview, trade **or deliberate no-trade**, a linked order, **revision history** (how the plan changed, not just its final state), and JSON export.

### Outcome attribution — the most valuable screen in the product
Instead of grading every result on P&L, the review separates three qualities:

1. **Thesis quality** — was the interpretation right?
2. **Execution quality** — did the order behave as planned?
3. **Process quality** — did the user follow the plan?

The system can classify outcomes like *"good thesis, bad execution"* or *"profitable trade, bad process"* — distinctions that actually make you better. A lucky win with a bad process is labelled what it is.

### Replay (Flight Simulator)
Historical replay done properly: starts at a **random hidden historical point** — future bars and future news are **hidden** — with separate modes (decision / execution / risk / **blind**), modelled latency, participation, spread and partial fills, stop handling including gap-through-stop, its own replay wallet, a buy-and-hold comparison, and results saved into research memory. It's a flight simulator for the market: fail safely, hundreds of times, before flying live.

### History & Verdict details
Past analyses, committee decisions with verdicts, risk labels, rationale and history — all preserved, nothing overwritten.

## 13. Research memory — Strategy Lab, Track Record, Research Network

This trio turns the platform from a trading app into an **operating system for personal research** — a capability that normally exists only inside institutional funds.

### Strategy Lab
Type a hypothesis in plain language — it is compiled into a **typed strategy definition** (with unparsed clauses shown honestly rather than guessed), then run through methodological quality gates: baseline comparison, **walk-forward analysis**, cost modelling, **placebo tests** (run it on data it shouldn't predict — if it "works", your method is wrong), sensitivity analysis, regime segmentation, concentration checks, data-quality checks, and shadow experiments. Verdicts are saved as versioned studies. This is how fraudulent backtests get caught *by design*.

### Track Record
Every forecast is treated as an experiment that **must state its outcome definition and wait for a mature evaluation window**. The system matures committee forecasts against real observed history by horizon, compares them against an honest constant baseline, and reports **accuracy, Brier scores, calibration and abstentions**, segmented by role, regime, sector, liquidity, coverage and model version. It also compares AI decisions with user plans, the initial thesis and execution quality. The platform's own honesty is on display here: with the recorded baseline, the AI committee's forecasts did *not* beat a naive constant — **and the product shows that** rather than hiding it.

### Research Network (Research Review)
Versioned research memos with cutoff, horizon and maturity, thesis, evidence, assumptions, **falsifiers** (what would prove this wrong), review status, and full revision history — exportable/importable as hash-verified (SHA-256) bundles, plus a matured-forecast leaderboard. It is an institutional-memory pattern for an individual: the platform remembers not only what you tried, but **why it changed or failed**. Team collaboration is explicitly labelled *gated* — not shipped-but-broken.

## 16. How the engineering is different

The differentiation isn't only features — it's how the machine is built:

- **Three planes with clear authority.** *Observation* (providers + validation code), *Research* (the agentic graph — opinions only), *Execution* (deterministic server code — money only). A persuasive model response cannot mutate a balance.
- **Server-authoritative everything.** Quotes, risk checks, fills, charges, settlement, worker — all server-side. The browser is a display and intent surface.
- **Typed, time-aware provenance.** The platform distinguishes five kinds of time: market/event time, publication time, first-seen time, available-at time, and calculation time — because "we calculated it now" does not make old data fresh. Every observation carries source, cutoff, confidence and freshness labels.
- **Durable worker.** A leased server worker (independent of the browser tab, dependent on the running app server) expires DAY orders, marks positions, fills resting orders, runs persisted protections and alerts, with savepoint-isolated operations.
- **Intentional storage duality.** A local-first JSON book (validated v2 schema, interprocess writer lock, atomic fsync writes, hashed migration backups) and an opt-in hosted InsForge PostgreSQL path (row-level security, owner-scoped reads, append-only history, admin-only commit authority, **compare-and-swap** commits with conflict retries) — the *same* engine and commands run on both. Also verified live: a fresh two-user isolation run with **29/29 checks passed**.
- **Local + hosted honesty.** Hosted mode fails closed — it never silently falls back to a local identity; practice mode is local-only and hidden in hosted mode.
- **A verification culture few projects match.** Typecheck, frontend/domain tests, intelligence tests, backend tests, Playwright browser tests (routes + accessibility), ESLint, production build, HTTP/perf benchmarks, accelerated soak runs, restore drills, fault-injection (concurrent writers, killed processes, orphaned locks, duplicate-intent floods, worker-lease takeover, revoked provider keys, stream failures), and hosted-isolation evidence.
- **Security posture.** Provider and model credentials stay server-side (`VITE_*` never carries secrets); paper mutations cross an authenticated server boundary; hosted auth keeps refresh tokens in HttpOnly cookies; offline mutations are refused.

## 18. What we deliberately do NOT do

The product treats its own honesty as a feature, so the boundaries are stated:

- **No live orders.** Paper only, by design — Fyers supplies observations, never execution. The AI never touches real capital; neither does the app.
- **NSE cash-equity delivery only.** Options, futures and BSE/global tradeables are gated, and the option-chain route says so out loud.
- **No calibrated prediction claims.** Conviction is labelled *uncalibrated opinion strength*. The recorded baseline runs did **not** beat a constant AI-free baseline — and Track Record displays that rather than hiding it. The platform's promise is a better *process*, not clairvoyance.
- **Sentiment scores are heuristics**, never probabilities. "70/100" is not "70% chance of rising".
- **No collaboration/multi-user teams yet** — explicitly gated in Research Network.
- **Corporate-action adjustments, FII/DII institutional flows, OCR of scanned filings** — named-unavailable in the Trust Control Room rather than faked.
- **Stale/delayed/missing data stays visible as that** — never silently converted into certainty.

This restraint is itself a differentiator: most trading products over-claim; this one makes underselling part of the design.

## 20. Glossary of simple terms

| Term | Plain meaning |
|---|---|
| **Paper trading** | Practising with virtual money on real market data. No real money moves. |
| **Paper book** | Quant Plus's virtual account ledger — cash, holdings, orders, fills, charges. |
| **Reservation** | Money/shares locked aside while an open order waits, so the same resources can't be spent twice. |
| **Settlement (T+1)** | In India, sale proceeds/shares become truly yours one trading day after the trade. The wallet respects that. |
| **Intent ID** | A receipt number for every account action — retrying can never create a duplicate. |
| **OCO** | One-Cancels-Other: a stop-loss and a target paired so filling one cancels the other. |
| **Quote observation** | A stored snapshot of exactly what the market looked like when a fill happened — not just the price. |
| **Fill method** | Whether your fill came from the bid/ask spread or from last trade plus slippage — recorded, not hidden. |
| **Research-only decision** | The committee's output: an opinion with evidence, governance-labelled, incapable of moving your account. |
| **Uncalibrated opinion strength** | A labelled confidence that explicitly is not a probability. |
| **Devil's advocate** | A dedicated AI stage whose only job is to attack the thesis before it becomes your decision. |
| **Ripple origin** | A verified, dated event in the Ripple Engine from which second-order effects are traced. |
| **Digital twin** | A what-if copy of your portfolio you can simulate changes on, without trading. |
| **Compare-and-swap (CAS)** | A database commit that refuses to overwrite data changed by someone else in between — prevents lost updates. |
| **Checkpoint** | A saved stage of the research pipeline so sessions can resume and completed work is never lost. |
| **Placebo test** | Running a strategy on data it shouldn't be able to predict — if it "wins", the method is broken. |
| **Fails closed** | When something is unavailable or unverified, the system refuses rather than guessing. |
